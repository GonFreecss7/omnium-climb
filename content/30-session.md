### 14. Session Structure

| Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 15 min | Easy movement, then V0–V1. Wrists, forearms, elbows, shoulders. |
| Technique | 20–30 min | One drill from §13, on easy problems. Do this fresh, not tired. |
| Main climbing | 40–50 min | One grade below your limit. Quality of movement over completion. |
| Projecting | 15 min | A few attempts at your limit, only when fresh and pain-free. |
| Cool-down | 10 min | Easy downclimbing, light stretching, antagonist work. |

**Antagonist work** (2–3×/week, 10 minutes): push-ups, rows, shoulder external rotation, wrist extensors and flexors. Climbers become pull-dominant fast and the joints pay for it.

### 15. Tracking Progress

Film one problem per session. Two things to look for:

1. How much time your arms spend bent.
2. How many times each foot moves after it's been placed.

Both numbers should trend down over weeks. That's technique improving, measurably.

**When to move up a grade.** The honest test for "this grade is boring": film yourself climbing it with one-touch and silent feet. If it's genuinely clean — no readjustments, no noise, no bent-arm hanging — move up. Most people find out it isn't.

**Session log — worth writing down**

- Which drill you used.
- Hardest problem climbed cleanly (not just completed).
- Any niggles: better / same / worse than last session.
- One technical thing that felt different.
