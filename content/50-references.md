### 17. References & Further Reading

#### 9 Out of 10 Climbers Make the Same Mistakes
- id: `ref-macleod-9-out-of-10`
- type: `book`
- author: Dave MacLeod — Rare Breed Productions, 2009
- note: How self-coached climbers get stuck — tactics, technique and training priorities. A good first book once the basics feel automatic.

#### Make or Break
- id: `ref-macleod-make-or-break`
- type: `book`
- author: Dave MacLeod — Rare Breed Productions, 2015
- note: Climbing injuries from prevention to rehab, written by a climber who has had most of them. Read it before the first injury, not after.

#### The Self-Coached Climber
- id: `ref-hague-hunter-self-coached`
- type: `book`
- author: Dan Hague & Douglas Hunter — Stackpole Books, 2006
- note: Movement mechanics in depth: balance, body position and how forces work on the wall. Pairs well with §3–§12.

#### The Climbing Bible
- id: `ref-mobraten-christophersen-bible`
- type: `book`
- author: Martin Mobråten & Stian Christophersen — Vertebrate Publishing, 2020
- note: Broad, modern overview of technique, strength, tactics and injury prevention for every level.

#### The Rock Climber's Training Manual
- id: `ref-anderson-rctm`
- type: `book`
- author: Michael L. Anderson & Mark Anderson — Fixed Pin Publishing, 2014
- note: Periodised training for route climbers, including the original repeater hangboard routine in §18.

#### Training for Climbing (3rd ed.)
- id: `ref-horst-training`
- type: `book`
- author: Eric J. Hörst — FalconGuides, 2016
- note: Wide-ranging training reference: physiology, strength, energy systems and planning.

#### MaxHangs and intermittent hangs
- id: `ref-lopez-maxhangs`
- type: `article`
- author: Eva López
- url: https://en-eva-lopez.blogspot.com/2018/03/maximal-hangs-intermittent-hangs.html
- note: The author's own explanation of maximal and intermittent dead-hangs — the source of the Max hangs protocol in §18.

#### Effects of two maximum grip strength training methods (2012)
- id: `ref-lopez-2012-sports-technology`
- type: `paper`
- author: López-Rivera & González-Badillo — Sports Technology 5(3–4)
- url: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/19346182.2012.716061
- note: Compares hangboarding on an 18 mm edge with added weight against the smallest edge at bodyweight, in experienced climbers.

#### Three hangboard programs and grip endurance (2019)
- id: `ref-lopez-2019-hukin`
- type: `paper`
- author: López-Rivera & González-Badillo — Journal of Human Kinetics 66
- url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6458579/
- note: Open-access follow-up comparing maximal, intermittent and combined dead-hang programs over eight weeks.

#### Basic hangboard routine (Hangboard FAQ)
- id: `ref-anderson-hangboard-faq`
- type: `article`
- author: Mark & Mike Anderson
- url: https://rockclimberstrainingmanual.com/2012/08/30/hangboard-faq-0-what-is-a-basic-hangboard-routine/
- note: The repeater routine explained step by step, with guidance on grips, sets and rest.

#### Finger strength self-assessment
- id: `ref-lattice-test`
- type: `article`
- author: Lattice Training
- url: https://latticetraining.com/app/uploads/2018/06/1528799872597_180503_Lattice-Testing-Training-Rung-Instructions.pdf
- note: The test protocol and the training percentages used by the finger test in §18.

#### Open or half crimp for performance profiling?
- id: `ref-lattice-half-crimp`
- type: `article`
- author: Lattice Training
- url: https://latticetraining.com/blog/open-or-half-crimp-for-performance-profiling-in-climbing
- note: Why half-crimp strength tracks boulder grade more closely than open-hand strength in Lattice's data.

#### Hooper's Beta
- id: `ref-hoopers-beta`
- type: `channel`
- author: Jason Hooper & Emile Modesitt
- url: https://www.hoopersbeta.com
- note: Physical-therapist-led videos on climbing injuries, rehab protocols and training myths.

#### Lattice Training
- id: `ref-lattice-channel`
- type: `channel`
- author: Lattice Training
- url: https://www.youtube.com/@LatticeTraining
- note: Coaching videos on assessment, finger strength and session planning.
