### 13. Drill Bank

Constraints make an easy problem hard without adding load to fingers and elbows. Pick **one** per session and apply it to V1–V3. Rotate so you're not always training the same weakness.

#### Footwork drills

- **Silent feet** — climb an easy problem with zero foot noise. Any scrape or slap, repeat the move.
- **Glue feet** — once a foot is placed it cannot move again until it leaves for the next hold. No shuffling.
- **Sniper** — chalk a small dot on each foothold; the toe must land on the dot.
- **Outside edge only** — every foot placement uses the little-toe edge. Forces backsteps and hip turns.
- **Feet eliminated** — remove the two best footholds from the problem. Same holds for hands, new puzzle.
- **Smear only** — on slab or vertical, no footholds at all; only the wall surface.
- **Foot swap** — every foothold must be used by both feet at some point, swapped without moving your hands.
- **Big-foot** — you may only use volumes and the biggest holds for feet. Forces long, committing steps.
- **Toe drag** — keep the trailing toe in contact with the wall through every move. Kills the dangling leg habit.

#### Body-position drills

- **Straight arms only** — if an elbow bends, you're off the problem. Forces backsteps and flags.
- **Hip kiss** — the hip must touch or nearly touch the wall on every move.
- **Flag hunt** — every move must include a flag of some kind. Ridiculous on some problems, which is the point.
- **Backstep everything** — use a backstep on every move that physically allows one.
- **No barn-door** — if your body swings open at any point, restart. Teaches you to feel it coming.
- **One-arm sections** — on easy jugs, remove one hand entirely for 2–3 moves and move with legs only.
- **One foot only** — climb an easy problem using a single foot; the other leg never touches a hold and works purely as a counterweight.

#### Movement drills

- **Slow motion** — 10 seconds per move. You can't hide bad balance behind momentum.
- **Hover** — hold the hand 5 cm from the target hold for 3 seconds before grabbing.
- **One-touch** — the hand lands on a hold and stays. No readjusting, no bumping.
- **Continuous flow** — no pause at all, metronome rhythm, top to bottom. The opposite of slow motion; train both.
- **Static then dynamic** — climb the same problem twice, once as statically as possible, once as dynamically as possible.
- **Downclimb everything** — climb up, then reverse it to the ground. Free footwork practice and it doubles your volume.
- **Deadpoint practice** — on an easy problem, make every hand move a small controlled pop rather than a reach.
- **Quiet hands** — no slapping, no re-gripping, no chalking mid-problem.
- **Grip awareness** — climb an easy problem consciously choosing a grip for each hold: open hand, half crimp, pinch, sloper. The goal is deliberate choice, not maximum grip.

> On grip work: bias heavily toward open hand and half crimp. Full crimping is the highest-stress grip for both fingers and elbow, and it is the last thing a beginner needs to train.

#### Reading & mental drills

- **Predict and verify** — write or say the full sequence out loud before pulling on, then check it against what actually happened.
- **Onsight only** — for one session, every problem gets exactly one attempt. Forces real reading.
- **Blind feet** — read the problem, then climb without looking down at your feet.
- **Reverse read** — read the problem from the top down before climbing it bottom-up.
- **Two solutions** — find and climb two genuinely different sequences for the same problem.

#### Partner games

- **Add-on** — you do move 1, partner adds move 2, alternate until someone falls.
- **Take-away** — alternate removing one hold from a problem until it becomes unclimbable. Last person to send wins.
- **Caller** — partner calls each hold out loud while you climb. You may only use what's called.
- **Mirror** — partner climbs a problem; you repeat it move for move, exactly, including foot placements.
- **Technique referee** — partner watches and counts errors: foot readjustments, bent arms, noisy feet. Lowest score wins.
- **Dice / roulette** — assign a constraint to each number, roll before each problem.

#### Volume & fun games

- **Traverse the wall** — link the start holds of every problem across a section.
- **Colour chaos** — pick one hold colour and climb anywhere on the wall using only that colour.
- **Ladder** — V1 → V2 → V3 → V2 → V1 back to back, then rest 5 minutes.
- **Circuit of four** — four V1–V2 problems in a row with no rest, then 3 minutes off. Repeat 3–4 times.
- **Pyramid session** — 4 problems at V1, 3 at V2, 2 at V3, 1 project. A complete session in itself.
- **Speed–quality** — climb a V1 as fast as possible, then as perfectly as possible. Compare how it felt.
- **No-repeat hour** — every problem you climb in the session must be one you've never done before.

> Circuits and traverses are pump-heavy. On a day when something already aches, choose a precision drill instead of a volume game.
