### 18. Finger Strength Training

Climbing itself trains the fingers, and for a long time it is the best finger training there is. Hangboard protocols add a measurable, controllable load once that base exists. Connective tissue adapts far more slowly than muscle (§1), so every protocol here assumes a thorough warm-up and stops the moment something hurts (§2).

:::level beginner
**Climb more before you hangboard.** Lattice Training recommends at least 6–12 months of consistent climbing before starting easier hangboard work. Until then, climbing on varied holds — and the drills in §13 — is the finger training.
:::

**Ground rules for every protocol**

- Warm up first: general movement, then progressively harder hangs on a big edge before the working sets.
- Use open hand or half crimp. Full crimp is the highest-stress grip for the fingers (§9) and stays out of hangboard work.
- Leave at least 48 hours between hangboard sessions, with no other hard finger training on those days.
- Stop the set on any sharp or one-sided finger pain. Adjust the load, never the rest.

#### Finger strength test
- id: `finger-test`
- level: `advanced`
- caution: true
- test: true
- timer: `prep 10, hang 7, rest 0, reps 1, sets 8, setrest 120`
- what: Lattice Training's self-assessment: the heaviest total load you can hold for a full 7-second two-arm dead hang on a 20 mm edge, expressed as a percentage of bodyweight. It is the number every load in a strength cycle is built from.
- how: After a rest day and a specific warm-up, do 4–8 sets of one 7-second hang, resting 2 minutes between sets. Start moderate and add load each set, aiming to reach your max within 8 sets. Keep the same grip (half crimp or four-finger open) for the whole test. If your fingers move, the set has failed — put your feet down. Retest with the same edge and grip before and after each training cycle.
- load: Total load = bodyweight + added weight (or minus weight removed with a pulley). Score = total load ÷ bodyweight × 100. Lattice designed it for climbers who already hangboard regularly and climb around V5 / 7b+ or harder.
- sources: https://latticetraining.com/app/uploads/2018/06/1528799872597_180503_Lattice-Testing-Training-Rung-Instructions.pdf, https://latticetraining.com/blog/open-or-half-crimp-for-performance-profiling-in-climbing

#### Repeaters 10/5
- id: `repeaters-10-5`
- level: `intermediate`
- caution: true
- timer: `prep 10, hang 10, rest 5, reps 6, sets 3, setrest 180`
- what: Sets of six 10-second hangs with 5-second rests — the Anderson brothers' entry-level repeater, gentler than 7/3.
- how: Pick a comfortable edge you can hold for every rep of the set. Rest 3 minutes between sets; one set per grip is the entry-level volume.
- load: Bodyweight or less — a plain hangboard can't go below bodyweight, so take weight off (feet on a chair, or a pulley) until the last rep is hard but still clean.
- sources: https://rockclimberstrainingmanual.com/2012/08/30/hangboard-faq-0-what-is-a-basic-hangboard-routine/, https://nicros.com/training/training-articles/research-on-hangboard-training/

#### Repeaters 7/3
- id: `repeaters-7-3`
- level: `advanced`
- caution: true
- timer: `prep 10, hang 7, rest 3, reps 6, sets 3, setrest 180`
- what: Sets of six 7-second hangs with 3-second rests, popularised by the Rock Climber's Training Manual. Trains strength-endurance with a grip–release rhythm close to climbing.
- how: Choose 3–7 grips and do one (entry) to three (advanced) sets per grip, resting 3 minutes between sets. Complete all sets of one grip before moving on.
- load: Add enough submaximal weight that each hang is challenging but the whole set can be completed.
- sources: https://rockclimberstrainingmanual.com/2012/08/30/hangboard-faq-0-what-is-a-basic-hangboard-routine/, https://trainingforclimbing.com/4-fingerboard-strength-protocols-that-work/

#### Max hangs
- id: `max-hangs`
- level: `advanced`
- caution: true
- timer: `prep 10, hang 10, rest 0, reps 1, sets 5, setrest 180`
- what: Single 10-second hangs at near-maximal load with long rests — Eva López's MaxHangs, studied in her doctoral research on an 18 mm edge.
- how: 3–5 sets of one 10-second dead hang, 3 minutes rest between sets. Half crimp on a fixed edge (around 18 mm).
- load: Add the weight that would let you hang about 13 seconds, but stop at 10 — always keep a ~3-second margin. If you can't finish a set with that margin, reduce the load.
- sources: https://en-eva-lopez.blogspot.com/2018/03/maximal-hangs-intermittent-hangs.html, https://en-eva-lopez.blogspot.com/2018/05

#### Minimum edge
- id: `min-edge`
- level: `advanced`
- caution: true
- timer: `prep 10, hang 12, rest 0, reps 1, sets 2, setrest 180`
- what: Eva López's bodyweight alternative to adding weight: hang on the smallest edge you can hold for about 15 seconds, but only for 12.
- how: 2 sets of one 12-second dead hang, 3 minutes rest between sets. As you get stronger, the edge gets smaller instead of adding weight.
- load: Bodyweight. The edge size is the load: smallest edge that allows ~15 seconds, hang 12 to keep the 3-second margin.
- sources: https://en-eva-lopez.blogspot.com/2018/05

#### Intermittent hangs
- id: `intermittent-hangs`
- level: `advanced`
- caution: true
- timer: `prep 10, hang 10, rest 20, reps 4, sets 3, setrest 120`
- what: The "long version" of Eva López's minimum-edge work: repeated 10-second hangs with short pauses, for strength that holds up over several efforts.
- how: 3 sets of 4 reps: 10-second dead hang, 20-second pause, 2 minutes between sets.
- load: The smallest edge that lets you complete every rep of all three sets.
- sources: https://en-eva-lopez.blogspot.com/2018/05
