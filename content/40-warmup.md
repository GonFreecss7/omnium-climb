### 16. Warm-up & Preparation

A warm-up has three jobs: raise tissue temperature, prepare the joints climbing loads hardest, and rehearse movement before intensity. In the session plan (§14) it takes about 15 minutes. The harder and steeper the session, the longer and more gradual the ramp should be.

**1. General — 3–5 minutes, off the wall**

Anything that raises your heart rate and makes you breathe a little harder: skipping, jumping jacks, stairs, light jogging on the spot. You should end up warm, not tired.

**2. Joints — 3–5 minutes**

- **Wrists:** slow circles both ways, then gentle flexion and extension with the palms on a wall.
- **Fingers:** open and close both hands fully and slowly, 10–15 times; spread the fingers wide between closes.
- **Forearms and elbows:** with the elbows bent, turn the palms up and down.
- **Shoulders:** arm circles from small to large. Then, on big jugs with straight arms and feet on the ground, draw the shoulder blades down and back a few times without bending the elbows.
- **Hips:** a deep squat hold, leg swings forward and to the side, a few hip circles.

**3. Movement — 5–10 minutes on the wall**

Climb problems well below your limit — two or three grades easier is about right — or traverse. Start on vertical or slab terrain and move toward steeper angles. Climb with quiet feet and straight arms: this is the session's first technique practice (§3, §4).

**4. Ramp — before anything near your limit**

Work up in steps: a few problems a grade or two easier, then one at your working grade, before the first attempt at your limit. Each step should feel clearly easier than the one after it. If a warm-up problem feels unusually hard, the session is telling you something — keep the day easier (§2).

:::level advanced+
**Finger ramp.** Before board sessions, small crimps or hangboard work (§18), add progressive hangs on a large edge: a few seconds each, feet on the ground or partly weighted at first, building load over several sets until a hang feels close to working intensity. Cold fingers are the ones that get hurt on the first hard crimp.
:::

**Short version — about 8 minutes**

When time is tight, keep the order and shorten each block: 2 minutes of general movement, one pass through the joints, then three or four easy problems that get steadily steeper. Leave limit attempts out of a short-warm-up session rather than cutting the warm-up further.

**Cool-down — 5–10 minutes**

Finish with easy climbing or downclimbing on big holds, then light stretching of the forearms and shoulders. This is also the place for the antagonist work in §14.
